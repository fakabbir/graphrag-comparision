\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\Cref{tab:main} gives the headline numbers and \Cref{tab:bytype} the per-category
breakdown. \Cref{fig:bytype} plots the latter and \Cref{tab:grid} shows every one
of the 180 attempts individually, unaggregated.

Summed over the question set, GraphRAG solved 16 of 20 questions on all three
attempts and passed 50 of 60 attempts; text-to-SQL solved 10 and passed 36;
vector RAG solved 3 and passed 9. These totals weight four hand-chosen categories
equally, and GraphRAG lost one of them outright, so the aggregate is a property of
this question mix rather than an overall ranking of the architectures. The
per-category results are the informative ones.

\subsection{By category}

\textbf{T1, single-document semantic.} GraphRAG passed 15 of 15. Vector RAG, the
expected winner, passed 3 of 15 --- all from one question --- with the other 12
attempts naming the right company and the right risks but citing no filing.
Text-to-SQL passed 7 of 15, using full-text ranking to locate the passage; its
remaining attempts were 6 uncited and 2 refusals. Note that both baselines here
fail predominantly by omitting provenance rather than by being wrong, which is a
materially different failure from asserting something false.

\textbf{T2, structured aggregation.} Text-to-SQL passed 15 of 15, as predicted.
GraphRAG passed 6 of 15, solving 2 of 5 questions, with 8 attempts returning
wrong answers and 1 refusing. Vector RAG passed 0 of 15: an aggregate over
10{,}511 companies is not present in any eight retrieved chunks, and it said so
rather than guessing. This is the one category the graph loses, and it loses it
badly and expensively.

\textbf{T3, multi-hop relational plus text.} GraphRAG passed 14 of 15; both
baselines passed 0 of 15. This is the category the design targets. It is also
where every one of the benchmark's confident falsehoods occurred, all six of them
text-to-SQL.

\textbf{T4, cross-document entity resolution.} GraphRAG passed 15 of 15 and
text-to-SQL 14 of 15; vector RAG passed 6 of 15. An earlier version of this
measurement recorded GraphRAG at 9 of 15 here and reported the category as a loss
for structural retrieval. That was a defect in our schema documentation rather
than a property of the graph; \Cref{sec:harness} and \Cref{tab:beforeafter}
describe the repair and its effect.

\subsection{Outcome composition}

\Cref{fig:outcomes} decomposes all 60 attempts per arm, and the composition
matters as much as the pass rate because the failure modes are not
interchangeable.

Vector RAG refused 39 times and was uncited 12 times. That is a low score but a
benign profile: asked about a company whose chunks it could not find, it either
abstained or answered without provenance, and it never asserted anything false.
Text-to-SQL was uncited 10 times and made \textbf{6 false statements}. GraphRAG
was never uncited and refused only once, which follows from its construction: the
traversal returns accession numbers before any text is read, so every claim
arrives with a key attached.

\subsection{Cost and latency}

\Cref{fig:cost} plots tokens against attempts passed. GraphRAG consumed 520{,}929
tokens to text-to-SQL's 144{,}068 and vector RAG's 118{,}185, giving it the
highest cost per passing attempt of the three (\Cref{tab:main}). Its accuracy is
bought, not free. The extreme case is T2, where GraphRAG spent 12.2 times
text-to-SQL's tokens to score 6 of 15 against 15 of 15 --- on the category it was
expected to lose.

The reverse cost is easy to overlook. \Cref{fig:latency} plots mean latency on a
log scale. Text-to-SQL averaged 10.0\,s against GraphRAG's 4.6\,s and vector
RAG's 1.3\,s, but the overall mean hides the shape: its worst single attempt took
274\,s, because the generated queries join \texttt{subsidiary},
\texttt{filing\_section} and \texttt{reporting\_owner} without narrowing first.
Unbounded LLM-authored SQL is a cost risk against one's own warehouse
independently of whether the answer is correct, which is why the live service
runs with a statement timeout that this measurement deliberately disabled
(\Cref{sec:harness}).

\subsection{Repair usage}

\Cref{tab:repair} and \Cref{fig:repair} report how often each arm used its single
repair allowance. Vector RAG has no query to repair and used none. As noted in
\Cref{sec:design}, the zero-row trigger is not neutral between the two query
languages: an over-broad SQL join returns many rows and never trips it, while an
exact graph property match that misses returns nothing and earns a free second
attempt. The direction of that bias favours the graph, and we report the usage
rather than claim it away.
